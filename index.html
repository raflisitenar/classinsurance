Insurance in 60 seconds
Insurance transfers certain financial risks to an insurer under a contract.

You pay a premium. In return, the insurer agrees to cover specified losses according to the policy's definitions, limits, deductibles, conditions and exclusions.

Coverage Library

Common types of insurance

Insurance products address different kinds of financial risk. Exact coverage varies by policy, provider and jurisdiction.

01

Auto Insurance

May address liability, vehicle damage, medical expenses and other eligible driving-related losses depending on the policy.

02

Home Insurance

Can help protect your home, belongings and personal liability against specified covered events.

03

Health Insurance

Helps pay eligible healthcare expenses according to plan rules, provider networks and cost-sharing requirements.

04

Life Insurance

Can provide a death benefit to named beneficiaries when policy requirements and covered conditions are met.

05

Travel Insurance

May cover specified trip cancellations, interruptions, delays, medical emergencies or baggage-related losses.

06

Business Insurance

A broad category that may address property, liability and certain operational risks faced by businesses.

Practical Guide

Five things to check before buying insurance

Understanding the contract can be more important than simply finding the lowest quoted premium.

1. Identify your actual financial risks

Consider which losses would create a meaningful financial burden and which risks you can reasonably retain yourself.

2. Understand the deductible

Know how much you may be responsible for and under what circumstances the deductible applies.

3. Check coverage limits

Limits determine how much a policy may pay for particular losses or during a policy period.

4. Read exclusions

Policies do not cover every event. Exclusions can materially change the protection you're purchasing.

5. Compare equivalent policies

When comparing prices, also compare limits, deductibles, exclusions, features and contract terms.

Policy Reading Workbook

Turn a policy into five answerable questions

Instead of reading an insurance document as one long block of legal language, record the contract in five fields. This does not determine whether a claim is covered; it gives you a repeatable way to identify what still needs clarification.

1. What event or risk triggers the coverage?

Find the coverage grant and note defined terms. An everyday word may have a narrower contractual definition.

2. What limit actually applies?

Look beyond the headline number for per-event limits, category limits, aggregate limits or sublimits that may apply to a particular loss.

3. What amount may remain your responsibility?

Record the deductible or other cost sharing and note when it applies. A deductible is different from the premium paid to maintain coverage.

4. What removes or modifies coverage?

Read exclusions together with endorsements. Endorsements can add, remove or change policy language, so a summary page alone may not tell the whole story.

5. What duties or conditions matter?

Note reporting, documentation, cooperation and other requirements. Ask the insurer or an appropriately licensed professional about language you do not understand.

Comparison Scenarios

Why matching prices or limits can mislead

A / LOWER PREMIUM

Higher deductible

A cheaper recurring premium can shift more of an eligible loss back to you. The useful comparison is the tradeoff, not price in isolation.

B / SAME LIMIT

Different contract

Two policies with the same headline limit can still differ in covered events, exclusions, sublimits, valuation, endorsements and conditions.

C / DAMAGE

Cause still matters

The existence of damage does not by itself establish coverage. The cause, circumstances and applicable policy language can materially affect the claim.

D / LIFE CHANGE

Coverage can age

A move, renovation, new driver, valuable purchase or change in use can be a reason to review whether existing coverage still matches the risk.

E / EXCLUSION

Read endorsements too

An endorsement may modify standard language. Compare the complete policy package rather than relying only on a marketing summary.

F / CLAIM

Prepare facts first

Build a factual timeline, preserve relevant records, note actions already taken and write down questions before the claim conversation.

Reference

Insurance glossary

Premium

The amount charged for insurance coverage.

Deductible

An amount the policyholder may need to pay before applicable benefits.

Coverage Limit

The maximum amount payable under specified policy terms.

Exclusion

A circumstance or type of loss that is not covered.

Claim

A request for coverage or payment under a policy.

Beneficiary

A person or entity designated to receive certain policy benefits.

Underwriting

The process insurers use to evaluate and price risk.

Policyholder

The person or entity that owns an insurance policy.

Liability

Legal responsibility for injury, damage or other loss.

Common Questions

Insurance FAQ

Is cheaper insurance always better?

No. A lower premium may come with different deductibles, limits, exclusions or coverage features. Compare equivalent policy terms rather than price alone.

Does insurance cover every loss?

No. Coverage depends on the policy contract and circumstances of the loss. Policies generally contain conditions, definitions, limits and exclusions.

Why do premiums vary?

Premiums can reflect the type and amount of coverage, underwriting factors, location, claims-related factors and other considerations permitted by applicable rules.

Should I review my coverage every year?

Periodic review can help identify whether changes in property, finances, family circumstances or other relevant factors affect your coverage needs.

What should I read before purchasing?

Review coverage, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions and relevant disclosures. Ask the insurer or a licensed professional about anything you do not understand.

Why can two policies with the same limit still be different?

The same headline limit can sit on top of different coverage grants, exclusions, sublimits, valuation methods, deductibles, endorsements and conditions.

What is an endorsement?

An endorsement modifies policy language and may add, remove or change coverage or conditions. It should be read together with the base policy.
